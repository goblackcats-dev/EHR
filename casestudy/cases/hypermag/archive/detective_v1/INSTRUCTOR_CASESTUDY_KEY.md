# INSTRUCTOR KEY: The Case of the Silent Mineral
*Not for students. Patient: Raymond "Ray" Kessler, 74, room 5E-14, Tue 11/17/2026 0700 to Wed 11/18/2026 0700. Student packet: `CASE_STUDY_PACKET.docx/.pdf`. Source of truth for the chart: `DESIGN.md` and `patient.json`; monitor settings and patient portrayal: `INSTRUCTOR_GUIDE.md` section 4.*

## How to use this key
- Print one student packet per squad (2 to 3 students). Open the EHR with Ray (Pre-Built Patients > Case Study Patients > Raymond Kessler) on the instructor screen, clock Tue 0700. Students use their own devices with the same patient and synced time.
- Open `monitor.html` on the projector (it follows the clock). Each checkpoint you press 4h, read the Situation, and the monitor changes to that checkpoint. The Bedside Nurse reads the vitals from the monitor and charts them.
- Squads rotate three jobs: A Chart Detective, B Lab Interpreter, C Bedside Nurse. Two-person squads: Desk (A+B) and Bedside (C), swapping each checkpoint; skip stretch boxes.
- The packet is 27 pages: cover, rubric, patient card, role cards, handoff, six checkpoint sections (3 pages each; the 1500 section is 4), and 3 Case Closed pages. Students must not read ahead.
- This key lists a model answer for every box: the answer, why (tied to the chart), what to look for, common misses. Full credit means complete and thoughtful, not word-for-word.

**Three things that go wrong:** (1) squads read ahead; (2) nobody asks Eleanor about OTC products at 0700 (release her line at about 0:16); (3) a student gives a magnesium product or sedative: pause and debrief it (guide section 9).

*Student prompt:* You put Ray on oxygen and the SpO2 climbs. Did that fix the problem? Why or why not?

- **Look for:** Look for "slow shallow breathing is the problem, not low oxygen".
- **Common misses:** Feeling reassured by a good SpO2.

#### 2A2  The antidote order
*Student prompt:* Find the PRN calcium gluconate order. Write: dose, how fast, and what the nurse must check before and during.
- **Model answer:** 1 g IV over 10 minutes, on the cardiac monitor, through a patent large vein (the 18 g right antecubital). Check the site and blood return before and during (calcium injures tissue if it leaks); watch HR, BP and rhythm; do not mix with other drugs in the line.
