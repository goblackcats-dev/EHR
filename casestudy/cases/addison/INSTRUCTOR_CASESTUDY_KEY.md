# Instructor Key: Lauren Mitchell Team Case Study (adrenal crisis)

## What this is

A 2-hour team case study. The whole class (designed for 7 students) works as ONE team and cares for Lauren Mitchell (46, adrenal crisis in known Addison disease after a stomach virus) across 24 hours of patient time, Mon 0700 to Tue 0700. The class advances 4 hours together (0700, 1100, 1500, 1900, 2300, 0300, then a final check at 0700). The packet is 12 pages: cover, patient card with a hormone map and two EHR screenshots, the rubric with a response tracker, one page per checkpoint, two final pages (evaluation, then teaching and discharge) and an individual reflection.

Focus: pathophysiology (cortisol and aldosterone missing: low sodium, high potassium, low volume, low glucose; high ACTH: dark skin), assessment, the standard treatment plan (stress-dose hydrocortisone on time, fluids, dextrose, fludrocortisone held then restarted), and evaluation of the response (the tracker every stop and the evaluation table at the end).

## Roles and pairs (7 students, one team)

- Charge nurse (rotates; each student has exactly one stop): runs the section, keeps time, asks each pair the printed "Charge asks" question, decides the priorities, makes the SBAR call and is the only one who writes in the shared boxes.
- Pair 1, Meds & Treatments (A, B): MAR, orders, notes: what is running, due, held or should change, and why.
- Pair 2, Vitals & Assessment (C, D): read the bedside monitor and chart the NOW row, orthostatic vitals, the bedside exam (mental status, skin, mouth, belly), intake and output.
- Pair 3, Results & Labs (E, F): new labs, ECGs, trends and what they mean (sodium rate, potassium, glucose, cortisol/ACTH).
- G, float nurse: helps where the charge nurse sends them (for example, fills the tracker column). G is charge at the final 0700.
- Charge order: A (0700), C (1100), E (1500), B (1900), D (2300), F (0300), G (final). When one of a pair is charge, the partner reports for the pair.

## Before class

- Print the shared packet single-sided: 2 or 3 copies for the table (or project the pages). Print 7 copies of page 12 (individual reflection and peer check).
- Load the EHR: Pre-Built Patients > Case Study Patients > Lauren Mitchell (or Import with casestudy/cases/addison/patient.json). Check that the clock reads Mon 07:00 (4W-22, SIM-40422).
- Open the bedside monitor (Monitor button) in the same browser; it loads Lauren's built-in scenario by MRN and follows the 4h button. The monitor BP is lying: you say the standing values aloud.
- There are no vital signs or bedside glucoses in the chart after 0700. You give the glucose and the standing values aloud (in each checkpoint section below). Ignore the instructor-only "Suggested vitals" bar on the Flowsheets page (it repeats the 0700 set).
- Optional moulage: bronzer on the face, neck and backs of the hands, darker palm creases; sweat spray at 1100.

## Rules that protect the pacing

- Students finish a page at the end of its time block. Nothing on a later page is printed on an earlier one (checked by check_facts.py). Do not let anyone turn the page until you advance the clock.
- Every number in this key matches the chart (patient.json), the instructor guide and the monitor. If the team and the key disagree, check the chart.
- Answers can differ from the model. Look for chart numbers and a reason, not exact wording.

- Charge nurse: asked each pair by name, kept time, made sure the tracker column was filled, kept the SBAR short and specific.

- Hormones: cortisol = low BP, glucose 49-58, nausea, belly pain, confusion, eosinophils 7%; aldosterone = Na 126, K 6.0 with peaked T, urine Na 48, dehydration, creatinine 1.6, salt craving; ACTH 1,240 = tan skin, palm creases, gums.
- Fludrocortisone: over about 50 mg/day hydrocortisone saturates the kidney enzyme and acts on the aldosterone receptor, so 200 mg/day covers both; at 50 mg/day oral it does not, so fludrocortisone restarts. Holding hydrocortisone brings the crisis back.
- Sodium: 126 to 132 = +6 in 9 h with about 260 mL/h pale urine (cortisol and volume back = ADH off = water diuresis). Osmotic demyelination 2-6 days later (speech, swallowing, weakness). Fix: lower-sodium fluid (D5 0.45% NaCl), D5W or desmopressin if needed.
- Evaluation: BP, orthostatics, HR and T waves, glucose, mental status, urine, Na and K: effective by 1900 (orthostatics negative, x4, eating, Na 133, K 4.4, creatinine 1.0).
- Potassium: fluids, dextrose (insulin moves K into cells), high-dose hydrocortisone acting like aldosterone, stool losses; fludrocortisone at 0800 lowers it further: KCl 20 mEq and BMP 1300.
- Teaching: double for 100.4 F or illness, triple for 102.2 F, inject 100 mg IM and call 911 for repeated vomiting or confusion, return demonstration by both, ID, card, extra tablets.

### Take-aways to close with

- Known adrenal insufficiency + vomiting = emergency; steroid first, tests never delay it.
- Hydrocortisone is time-critical: on time, never held for "good" numbers.
- High-dose hydrocortisone covers aldosterone too; fludrocortisone comes back at 50 mg/day.
- Evaluate with data: BP and orthostatics, HR and T waves, glucose, mental status, urine, sodium and potassium.
- A rising sodium can be dangerous: count the change from the first value and the hours; watch the urine.
- Falls happen when patients feel better. Teach to prevent the next crisis.
