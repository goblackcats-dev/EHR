## Teaching points for the debrief
1. **Why pump users get DKA fast**: rapid-acting insulin only, no depot; 2-4 hours after delivery stops, ketones climb. A good A1c does not protect against a failed set.
2. **The unexplained-high rule**: glucose over 250 not falling 1 hour after a pump correction (or any ketones, nausea, vomiting) = check ketones, **inject by pen**, change the whole set at a new site, recheck. A pulled-out or leaking set does NOT trigger an occlusion alarm. Never correct twice through a set you do not trust.
3. **Sick-day rules for pump users**: glucose every 2-3 h, ketones every 2-4 h, never stop basal, fluids, ketones 0.6-1.4 = pen + set change + call if not falling; 1.5 or more or vomiting = call / ED. **Vomiting in type 1 diabetes is DKA until ketones say otherwise**, even after a night of drinking.
4. **Backup kit**: glargine pen (20 units if the pump is off more than about 3 hours and cannot be fixed; restart the pump about 24 hours later), lispro pen, needles, ketone meter and strips, spare sets, glucagon; check expiry dates.
5. **Alcohol and type 1 diabetes**: the liver stops releasing glucose while it clears alcohol (delayed lows up to 24 h); drinking hides lows, highs and alarms; eat carbs, bolus less for alcohol carbs, bedtime glucose and snack, alarms loud and phone charged, a friend who knows the plan, medical ID. Ethanol level and AKA overlap; thiamine before dextrose; CIWA-Ar screening (negative); SBIRT brief intervention.
6. **Potassium with peripheral IVs**: riders only, max 10 mEq/h; K under 3.5 = hold insulin (and the dextrose), replace, restart when 3.5+. Low magnesium makes low potassium hard to fix.
7. **Resolution is about ketones**: pH reached 7.30 at 2130 Monday with BHB 2.0, not resolved. Transition with overlap; lows after the transition (insulin needs fall; dose after eating if intake is uncertain).
8. **Working with a young adult expert patient**: he knows his diabetes better than most staff; respect that while holding safe limits (pump restart after review, sugar-free fluids on the drip).
