# EVIDENCE: adult DKA reference sheet for the in-class patient

Reference for the clinical reviewer to check `DESIGN.md` against. No patient numbers. Main source: the **2024 ADA/EASD/JBDS/AACE/DTS consensus report "Hyperglycemic crises in adults with diabetes"** (as summarized in `../DKA_SERIES.md` and used in dka1-dka7). The full text could not be fetched in this session (PubMed returned the abstract only; the publisher and PMC sites were blocked), so details written from knowledge are marked **(verify)**. Where the 2024 consensus leaves a choice to local protocol, this sheet says so. Glucose in mg/dL; K in mEq/L.

## 1. Diagnosis and severity (adult DKA, 2024 consensus)
- **D**: glucose 200 or higher, OR a known history of diabetes (any glucose; euglycemic DKA counts).
- **K**: beta-hydroxybutyrate (BHB) 3.0 mmol/L or higher (preferred), OR urine ketones 2+ or higher.
- **A**: pH under 7.3 and/or bicarbonate under 18 mmol/L. Anion gap (Na - Cl - HCO3, normal 8-12) supports the diagnosis but is not part of it.
- **Severity** (classic table kept in 2024 (verify)): mild pH 7.25-7.30 / HCO3 15-18; moderate pH 7.00-7.24 / HCO3 10-14.9; severe pH under 7.00 / HCO3 under 10 or stupor/coma. Features that point to ICU/step-down level (verify wording): pH under 7.0, BHB over 6, K under 3.5 at arrival, low GCS, systolic BP under 90, HR over 100 or under 60, anion gap over 16. A patient can be "moderate by pH, severe by bicarbonate": the notes should say which.
- Always look for the precipitant (here: insulin omission during a viral illness) and check for infection, MI (ECG, troponin if indicated), pregnancy test when relevant.

## 2. Fluids
- **First hour**: isotonic crystalloid **1-1.5 L** (0.9% NaCl or a balanced fluid such as lactated Ringer's; the 2024 report allows either and notes balanced fluids may resolve DKA a little faster with less high-chloride acidosis). Smaller boluses (250-500 mL with reassessment) for heart or kidney disease.
- **Then 250-500 mL/h** guided by volume status, BP, urine output and labs (verify the exact range; many protocols use about 250 mL/h after 2-4 h). Aim to replace the estimated deficit over 24-48 h.
- **0.45% NaCl**: after initial resuscitation, when the **corrected sodium is normal or high** (verify the 2024 wording; the 2009 ADA algorithm used this rule). Stay on 0.9% if corrected Na is low.
- **Corrected sodium** = measured Na + **1.6 x (glucose - 100) / 100** (some use 2.4; ISPAD uses 2). A rising corrected Na means free-water deficit; a falling measured Na while glucose falls is a warning for too much free water.
- **Dextrose**: add **D5 (or D10) when glucose falls below 250**, run with the saline ("two-bag" method or separate line), and keep the insulin running. Purpose: let the insulin clear the ketones without hypoglycemia. Glucose normalizes well before ketosis does.
- Lactated Ringer's carries 4 mEq/L K: count it against the local peripheral K limit (section 4).

## 3. Insulin
- **Regular insulin IV infusion 0.1 unit/kg/h** (fixed, weight-based). A **priming bolus of 0.1 unit/kg is optional** (not required if the infusion starts at 0.1 unit/kg/h; 2024 says either (verify)). Older alternative: 0.14 unit/kg/h without a bolus.
- Do **not start insulin until K is 3.5 or higher** (section 4). Give fluids first if K is unknown.
- Expected glucose fall **50-75 mg/dL/h**; if glucose falls less than that (or less than 10% in the first hour, or ketones do not fall) check the line and the pump, then the prescriber increases the rate.
- **Below 250**: add dextrose and **either lower insulin to 0.05 unit/kg/h** (ADA style) **or keep the fixed 0.1 unit/kg/h and add D10** (JBDS style); local protocol chooses. Keep glucose about **150-200** (verify; some protocols 140-200) until DKA resolves.
- **If glucose drops below target**: first **raise the dextrose** (D5 -> D10, or faster rate); lower the insulin rate only per protocol and never stop it while ketones persist, because stopping insulin restarts ketogenesis. Under 70 = hypoglycemia rescue (section 8).
- **Hold insulin for K under 3.5** (at start, and many protocols also during treatment); replace K and restart when K is 3.5 or higher (local rule: with prescriber confirmation).
- Mild uncomplicated DKA may be treated with SC rapid-acting insulin every 1-2 h on a general ward (2024 option); not for this moderate-severe case.
- Continue the patient's **basal insulin** early (0.15-0.3 unit/kg or home dose SC while on the drip) is allowed by the 2024 report to shorten the infusion and prevent rebound (verify dose wording).

## 4. Potassium
**Consensus** (verify exact mEq):
| Serum K | Action |
|---|---|
| Under 3.5 | **Hold insulin**; give KCl (about 10-20 mEq/h) until K is 3.5 or higher |
| 3.5-5.0 | Add K so the serum stays about 4-5 (consensus writes 20-30 mEq per liter of fluid) |
| Over 5.0 | No K; recheck every 2 h |
Give K only with urine output (about 0.5 mL/kg/h or more). Cardiac monitor for any K rider and for K under 3.5 or over 5.5.

**Instructor's local peripheral rule (all patients; overrides "20-30 mEq/L in the bag")**
- Peripheral IV: **no more than 10 mEq/h of potassium in total**, every minute.
- Give as **KCl 10 mEq/100 mL riders on a pump, one at a time, over 1 h; at most 4 in a row (40 mEq), then recheck K**.
- **No more than 20 mEq/L in a peripheral bag.** **40 mEq/L is the central-line cap.**
- **Never stack a rider on a K-containing carrier above 10 mEq/h total** (e.g., 20 mEq/L at 150 mL/h = 3 mEq/h, plus a 10 mEq/h rider = 13: not allowed; switch the carrier to K-free while the rider runs).
- Riders add 100 mL/h: chart as IV med on the I&O.

## 5. Phosphate, magnesium, bicarbonate
- **Phosphate**: falls with insulin; routine replacement not helpful. Replace when **under 1.0 mg/dL** or with heart failure, respiratory weakness or hemolysis (K phosphate 20-30 mEq/L in the consensus; locally as a slow rider within the 10 mEq/h K limit). Count its K.
- **Magnesium**: not in the consensus algorithm (verify); common practice replaces low Mg (e.g., under 1.7-1.8 mg/dL) because low Mg makes low K hard to correct (Mg sulfate 2 g IV).
- **Bicarbonate**: **not recommended unless pH is under 7.0** (2024; some protocols say under 6.9). If given: e.g., 100 mmol NaHCO3 in 400 mL water with KCl over 2 h, repeat until pH 7.0 (verify). Risks: low K, worse intracellular acidosis, delayed ketone clearance.

## 6. Monitoring (nurse frequencies)
- **POC glucose every hour** on the insulin infusion (every 30 min if falling fast or near 70), then before meals and at bedtime (and 0300 the first night) after transition.
- **BMP (+ Mg, phos) every 2-4 h**; **VBG pH/HCO3 and BHB every 2-4 h** until resolution (venous is enough; arterial not needed). Anion gap each BMP.
- **K 1-2 h after insulin starts** and after any rider set (local: after 4 riders).
- **Cardiac monitor/telemetry** on admission and while K is out of range or riders run; **12-lead ECG** at arrival and for K under 3.5 (flat T, U waves) or over 5.5 (peaked T). A normal ECG does not rule out abnormal K.
- **Vitals** every 1 h at first (every 15 min during the first hour of fluids per local policy), then every 2-4 h; **strict I&O** hourly urine; **daily weight**.
- **Neuro checks** (LOC, GCS, headache, pupils) every 1-2 h for the first 12-24 h or while severe.
- Lungs and edema after each liter (fluid overload).

## 7. Resolution and transition to subcutaneous insulin
- **Resolved (2024)**: **venous pH 7.3 or higher OR bicarbonate 18 or higher, AND BHB under 0.6 mmol/L** (verify; glucose is no longer part of the definition). If BHB is not available: glucose under 200 and anion gap 12 or less plus pH/HCO3. A closed gap with BHB still high is NOT resolved.
- **Transition**: patient able to eat. Give **SC basal insulin before stopping the infusion: 2 h overlap minimum** (2024 says 2-4 h (verify); the series used 1-2 h, dka6/dka7 used 2 h). Give a rapid-acting meal dose with the first meal. Stop the infusion and the dextrose together after the overlap.
- **Dose**: known type 1 on injections: **resume the home basal-bolus doses** if control was reasonable (adjust for HbA1c, intake, kidney function). Insulin-naive or uncertain: **0.5-0.6 unit/kg/day**, about half basal and half mealtime split over three meals, plus a correction scale (lower, 0.3-0.4 unit/kg/day, for thin, older or kidney disease) (verify).
- After transition: BMP + BHB once to check for rebound ketosis, then daily.

## 8. Complications on treatment and what the nurse does
| Problem | Signs | Nurse actions |
|---|---|---|
| **Hypoglycemia** (under 70; most common) | shaky, sweaty, confused, or none | Treat now: alert and eating -> 15 g fast carbohydrate (avoid high-K juice if K is high); NPO or not alert -> D50 25 g (or D10 per order) IV; glucagon 1 mg IM if no IV. Recheck in 15 min, repeat until 100+. On the drip: increase dextrose per protocol, do not just stop the insulin. Call provider. |
| **Hypokalemia** | weakness, cramps, flat T/U waves, PVCs | Hold insulin if K under 3.5; riders per the local rule; telemetry; ECG; recheck K; check Mg. |
| **Rebound ketosis** | nausea, gap reopens, BHB rises after the drip stops | Prevented by the basal overlap; check BHB/BMP after transition; report; may need the drip again. |
| **Fluid overload** | crackles, falling SpO2, edema, weight gain | Sit up, O2, slow fluid, call provider; watch older adults and heart/kidney disease. |
| **Hyperchloremic acidosis** | low HCO3 with a closed gap | Not DKA; balanced fluid; no extra insulin. |
| **Cerebral edema** (rare in adults, mainly under 20) | headache, vomiting returns, drowsiness, slowing HR with rising BP, pupils | Head up 30 degrees, call rapid response, slow fluids, mannitol 0.5-1 g/kg or 3% saline per order. |
| Others | thrombosis, aspiration with vomiting, line infiltration | Mobilize, NG if vomiting and drowsy, check IV sites. |

## 9. Sick-day rules (type 1 on injections) and discharge teaching
- **Never stop basal insulin**, even when not eating (the misconception in this case). Keep mealtime insulin matched to what is eaten.
- **Glucose every 2-4 h** and **blood (or urine) ketones every 2-4 h** when ill or glucose is over 250.
- **Correction insulin** with rapid-acting insulin per the plan; with ketones, extra **10-20% of the total daily dose every 2-4 h** is a common rule (verify against the local diabetes team plan).
- **Fluids**: sugar-free fluids about 120-250 mL every hour; if not eating, sugar-containing fluids or easy carbohydrate (about 15 g every hour or 45-50 g every 3-4 h) so insulin can be given.
- **Go to the ED / call** when: vomiting and cannot keep fluids down (more than about 2-4 h), blood ketones 1.5 or higher not falling after extra insulin (3.0 or higher = ED) or urine ketones moderate-large, glucose over 300 that does not fall after 2 corrections, belly pain, fast or deep breathing, confusion, or unable to manage.
- Keep ketone strips, glucagon, backup supplies; a written sick-day plan; tell a roommate/friend.
- **Discharge readiness**: DKA resolved and stable on SC insulin (no drip about 12-24 h (verify local)), eating and drinking, K and glucose stable, no hypoglycemia pattern, teach-back done (sick-day rules, injection technique, pen/needle, hypoglycemia 15-15 rule, glucagon, ketone testing), insulin and supplies in hand and affordable, follow-up with the diabetes team in 1-2 weeks (sooner by phone), primary care.

## 10. Droplet precautions for influenza-like illness
- **Start**: droplet precautions (surgical mask within about 6 feet, private room, patient masks for transport) for suspected influenza while the respiratory viral panel (influenza A/B, COVID-19, RSV) is pending. Note: CDC advises COVID-19 suspicion uses N95 + eye protection (verify the local policy and whether the in-class build simplifies this to droplet).
- **Confirmed influenza**: precautions for **7 days after illness onset or until 24 h after fever and respiratory symptoms resolve, whichever is longer** (CDC).
- **Discontinue** when the panel is **negative** and there is no other indication (local policy wording, suggested chart text): *"Droplet precautions may be discontinued when the respiratory viral panel is negative for influenza A/B, SARS-CoV-2 and RSV and clinical suspicion is low. Continue standard precautions, respiratory hygiene and a mask for the patient if coughing."* The nurse documents the result, the reason, removes the sign, updates the isolation order (if the order requires a provider or infection-prevention order to stop, get it). A negative panel does not rule out other viruses (e.g., rhinovirus): standard precautions and cough etiquette continue.

## 11. Nursing priorities and SBAR examples
**Priorities**: (1) airway, breathing, LOC; (2) fluids and perfusion; (3) potassium safety before and during insulin; (4) insulin infusion, hourly glucose, dextrose step; (5) labs every 2-4 h, gap and BHB to resolution; (6) safe transition with overlap; (7) hypoglycemia prevention; (8) infection control; (9) sick-day teaching and discharge readiness.

- **Arrival handoff**: S: 28-year-old with type 1 in moderate-severe DKA from the ED. B: stopped basal insulin during a viral illness; fluids and insulin started overnight. A: (pH, HCO3, gap, BHB, K, glucose trend, IV sites, rates, urine output, droplet precautions, panel pending). R: next labs due at ___, hourly glucose, confirm infusion rates and pump programming at the bedside.
- **K under 3.5 on insulin**: S: K is ___ (under 3.5). B: on insulin ___ units/h, peripheral IV only. A: flat T/U waves on monitor, urine ___ mL/h. R: insulin held per protocol; request KCl 10 mEq/100 mL riders one at a time (max 4, K-free carrier), Mg level, recheck K after the riders and a restart order at K 3.5 or higher.
- **Glucose under 250**: S: glucose ___. B: insulin ___ units/h, saline ___ mL/h. A: gap ___, BHB ___ (still ketotic). R: start dextrose per protocol and confirm whether the insulin rate is lowered to 0.05 unit/kg/h or kept.
- **Hypoglycemia**: S: glucose ___ (under 70), symptomatic/not. B: on insulin ___ / after transition. A: treated with ___ at ___, recheck ___. R: adjust dextrose or the next insulin dose; review basal dose.
- **Resolution/transition**: S: DKA resolved (pH ___ / HCO3 ___, BHB ___). B: eating. A: K ___, glucose ___. R: order SC basal (home dose ___ or weight-based) now, stop the infusion 2 h after it is given, meal insulin with the meal, BMP + BHB after transition.
- **Panel negative**: S: respiratory panel negative. B: droplet precautions since the ED for flu-like illness. A: afebrile/cough ___, no other indication. R: discontinue droplet precautions per policy (order if required); continue standard precautions and cough etiquette.
