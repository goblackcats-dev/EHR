## Teaching points
1. **A normal-looking glucose does not exclude DKA** (glucose 198 at home, 210 in the ED): SGLT2 inhibitors cause glycosuria, keep glucose low and ketones high. Check blood ketones with nausea, vomiting, deep breathing or confusion; never skip basal insulin; do not restart any SGLT2 inhibitor; urine glucose stays strongly positive for days.
2. **Respiratory compensation (Winter's formula)**: arterial pCO2 29 against an expected 21.5 +/- 2 means she is not keeping up; reassess airway and fatigue. Venous gases are not read with Winter's.
3. **DKA resolves on ketones and acid-base**, not the glucose or the gap: BHB under 0.6 plus pH 7.3 or higher or bicarbonate 18 or higher; give basal insulin 2 hours before stopping the drip.
4. **Potassium on insulin**: K 3.3 means hold, replace, restart at 3.5 or higher with the prescriber; peripheral limit 10 mEq/h from all sources, LR counts (4 mEq/L); one rider at a time; Mg and phosphorus are checked, phosphorus is replaced only under 1.0 or with signs.
5. **Sepsis care**: cultures before the antibiotic, antibiotic within the hour, 30 mL/kg with reassessment, lactate recheck, culture-directed narrowing. Count every IV volume; D10W is free water.
6. **Allergy safety**: a culture report can list a drug she cannot take; check the allergy list at every dose.
7. **Delirium and falls**: treat causes, orient, assist, protect sleep, avoid benzodiazepines and restraints; hands-on assistance beats alarms.
8. **Hypoglycemia unawareness**: treat a low glucose without symptoms; CGM low alert 90 with sharing; glucagon (nasal) for Emi; no driving until reviewed; avoiding lows can restore warning symptoms.
9. **VTE prophylaxis follows risk**: here it is justified (sepsis, ICU, reduced mobility, age); contrast with the younger ambulatory patients in the series.
