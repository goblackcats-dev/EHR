## Teaching points for the debrief
1. **DKA with a normal-looking pH**: bicarbonate 9 and BHB 7.2 define severity; pH 7.23 alone understates it. Gap closure and pH 7.30 are not resolution: **BHB under 0.6 is required** (0745 pH 7.30 / BHB 3.5; 1145 pH 7.33 / BHB 1.6).
2. **Hold insulin when K is under 3.5**; insulin drives potassium into cells (5.3 -> 3.2 in 6 hours). Replace, recheck early, restart at 3.5 or higher with provider confirmation. Telemetry: flat T and U waves.
3. **Peripheral IVs limit potassium to 10 mEq/h total**: no 40 mEq/L bags, one piggyback at a time, no potassium-containing carrier while piggybacks run, and **magnesium on the other line** (low Mg makes low K hard to fix).
4. **Phosphate 1.5 is not replaced** (replace under 1.0, or with heart or breathing compromise or hemolysis; it recovers with eating). Watch for weakness; monitor each panel.
5. **Dextrose AND lower insulin at glucose under 250**, but never stop it before the ketones clear (1200: glucose 220, BHB 1.6).
6. **Transition**: glargine 2 hours before the infusion stops; the old 2100 basal dose is dropped; meal dose only with food.
7. **Fixed meal doses are risky when appetite is uncertain**: dose to the carbohydrate actually eaten (1 unit per 15 g) and ask before the dose. Treat the low (rule of 15), find the cause, keep the basal.
8. **Cost-related rationing is a safety problem, not a character flaw**: use respectful language, ask about food, verify **actual insulin, strips, needles, glucagon, a ride and a follow-up in hand**, and teach with the real products (pen mix-up). A savings card, an application, or a coupon is not a plan by itself; know who owns the renewal.
9. **Sick-day rules for someone who cannot eat**: never stop basal insulin; ketones when ill or glucose 250 or higher; fluids; call early.
10. **VTE prophylaxis is not ordered**: she is 38, BMI 22 and ambulatory with no clot-risk factor; ask students when it would be (immobility, obesity, prior clot, pregnancy, MI per protocol).
