## 2. Story before the ED and ED course

### Pre-Arrival Narrative
Thirty-six hours before Emergency Department (ED) arrival, while finishing his afternoon bus route, Samuel developed a dull, persistent epigastric ache accompanied by unusual, heavy fatigue and mild diaphoresis. Because of his history of GERD and long-standing T1DM, he attributed the symptoms to indigestion and a mild viral stomach bug, unaware that he was experiencing a "silent" non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) masked by diabetic autonomic neuropathy. By the morning prior to admission, he felt nauseated and anorexic. Remembering outdated misconceptions that taking insulin without eating causes severe hypoglycemia, he skipped his morning and afternoon insulin lispro doses and omitted his bedtime insulin glargine ($24\text{ units}$) at 2100. Unopposed counter-regulatory catecholamine and cortisol surges from his acute myocardial ischemia, combined with absolute insulin deficiency, rapidly accelerated lipolysis and ketogenesis. By 2330, he developed intractable nausea, two episodes of non-bloody bilious emesis, polyuria, unquenchable polydipsia, and diffuse abdominal cramping. At 0100, his wife Grace noticed he was breathing deeply and rapidly, looked pale and exhausted, and had a fruity odor on his breath; she drove him immediately to the hospital ED.

### Emergency Department Timeline (Day 1: 0130 – 0700)

Two 18-gauge peripheral IV catheters inserted (left forearm and right antecubital fossa).

* **0210:** Lab results return confirming moderate-to-severe DKA (Glucose $540\text{ mg/dL}$, $\text{Na}$ $131\text{ mEq/L}$, $\text{K}$ $5.2\text{ mEq/L}$, $\text{Cl}$ $95\text{ mEq/L}$, $\text{HCO}_3$ $12\text{ mEq/L}$, Anion Gap $24\text{ mEq/L}$, Venous $\text{pH}$ $7.21$, BHB $5.8\text{ mmol/L}$) and acute myocardial injury ($\text{hs-cTnI}$ $185\text{ ng/L}$; normal $< 14\text{ ng/L}$). * **0245:** $1000\text{ mL}$ $0.9\%\text{ NaCl}$ bolus finishes. * **0430:** Repeat labs drawn (3-hour troponin trend and 3-hour DKA panel). Second $\text{hs-cTnI}$ rises sharply to $640\text{ ng/L}$, confirming dynamic NSTEMI. Serum potassium has dropped from $5.2$ to $4.3\text{ mEq/L}$ as insulin drives potassium intracellularly.

Atorvastatin $80\text{ mg}$ PO administered. Oral beta-blocker is explicitly deferred by Cardiology until tachycardia and volume depletion from DKA resolve.
* **0630:** Patient reports epigastric discomfort has improved from $4/10$ to $1/10$, nausea resolved. Report called to the Cardiac Step-Down / Telemetry Unit nurse.
* **0700:** Arrives on Cardiac Step-Down Unit (Bed 412).

### Core Nursing & Interprofessional Teaching Points

1. **Recognizing "Silent" / Atypical Myocardial Ischemia in Diabetes:**
   * Because long-standing Type 1 Diabetes damages autonomic nerve fibers, heart attacks often do **not** cause classic crushing left-sided chest pain. Instead, ischemia can feel like **"indigestion," dull epigastric ache, unexplained heavy fatigue, nausea, cold sweats, jaw/back ache, or sudden shortness of breath**.
   * **Action Plan:** If any of these symptoms occur, stop activity, sit down, check blood glucose (to rule out hypoglycemia), place one **$0.4\text{ mg}$ Nitroglycerin tablet under the tongue**, and **call 911** if symptoms are not gone within 5 minutes.

2. **Type 1 Diabetes "Sick-Day Rules" (Preventing Recurrent DKA):**
   * **Golden Rule:** **Never omit basal insulin (Insulin Glargine $22\text{ units}$)**, even when nauseated, vomiting, or fasting. During illness or stress, the body releases stress hormones (adrenaline and cortisol) that raise blood sugar and break down fat into acidic ketones even when you eat nothing.
   * **Monitoring:** Check blood glucose and **blood beta-hydroxybutyrate ketones** (using the prescribed home blood ketone meter) every **2 to 4 hours** during any illness, infection, or whenever blood glucose is $> 250\text{ mg/dL}$.
   * **Fluid & Carbohydrate Replacement:** Drink 4 to 8 oz of fluid every hour to prevent dehydration. If blood glucose is $> 200\text{ mg/dL}$, drink water, broth, or sugar-free electrolyte drinks. If blood glucose is $< 200\text{ mg/dL}$ and you cannot eat solid food, sip carbohydrate-containing liquids (sports drinks, apple juice, regular soda, crackers) while continuing insulin.
   * **Emergency Triggers:** Seek immediate medical care if blood ketones reach **$\ge 1.5\text{ mmol/L}$**, blood glucose stays $> 300\text{ mg/dL}$ after two correction doses, or vomiting prevents keeping liquids down for $> 2\text{ hours}$.

3. **Stent Protection, Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT), and Bleeding Precautions:**
   * The drug-eluting stent in the OM1 coronary artery requires **both Aspirin $81\text{ mg}$ daily and Ticagrelor $90\text{ mg}$ twice daily for at least 12 months** to prevent a fatal blood clot inside the metal stent.
   * Because DAPT thins the blood, use a soft toothbrush, electric razor, and avoid NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen). Call the cardiologist or go to the ED for black tarry stools, blood in urine, severe headache, or nosebleeds lasting $> 15\text{ minutes}$. Note that mild transient shortness of breath can occasionally occur as a benign side effect of ticagrelor, but any new dyspnea should still be evaluated.

4. **Right Radial Artery Access-Site Care:**
   * Keep the right wrist clean and dry; shower is permitted, but do not soak the wrist underwater (no baths or swimming) for 5 days.
   * Avoid lifting more than **5 pounds**, pushing up from chairs, or vigorous repetitive wrist motion with the right arm for 1 week.
   * **Emergency Care:** If bleeding or a sudden expanding, throbbing lump develops at the right wrist, apply firm direct pressure with two fingers just above the puncture site and **call 911**.

5. **Beta-Blocker & Hypoglycemia Awareness + CDL Return-to-Work Pathway:**
   * **Beta-Blocker Warning:** Metoprolol blunts the warning symptom of a racing heart (tachycardia) and shakiness during low blood sugar, but **sweating (diaphoresis) is NOT masked**.
