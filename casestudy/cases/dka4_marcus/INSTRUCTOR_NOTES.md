## Teaching points
1. **Kidney disease changes the usual DKA playbook.** Small, reassessed boluses; count riders, flushes and insulin carrier as intake; urine under 40 mL/h (0.5 mL/kg/h) for 2 hours is a call; low output plus crackles is not a bolus indication.
2. **Potassium in AKI.** It falls with insulin even though the kidneys clear it poorly; the hold threshold is 3.5; replace one small bag at a time, only with adequate urine, with an ECG, a recheck and the prescriber's authorization for each bag; never above 10 mEq/h through peripheral IVs. The value of exactly 3.5 after one bag is not a restart signal.
3. **DKA resolves on ketones, not on the gap or the pH alone** (BHB under 0.6 plus pH 7.3 or higher or bicarbonate 18 or higher). The gap is normal-ish at 1500 and the pH is 7.35; BHB is still 1.0.
4. **Phosphate** is monitored (1.7), not replaced without symptoms or a value under 1.0; potassium phosphate carries potassium.
5. **Automatic eGFR is not measured GFR.** It is shown for trend and labelled; drug doses use the creatinine, urine output and pharmacy review. Weight and lung findings, not the I&O sum alone, guide fluids.
6. **Renal insulin clearance and the low glucose.** Basal reduced 24 -> 18 -> 14; correction spacing 4 hours; no bedtime correction; basal is never omitted; treat lows with apple juice or tablets (lower potassium than orange juice).
7. **Normal ECG does not clear potassium** (5.8 and 5.2 with normal T waves) and flat T with small U fits the low value.
8. **Sick-day insulin**: basal continues without food; check ketones; call early. Kidney protection: losartan held until nephrology reviews the Monday 0900 BMP, no NSAIDs, no potassium salt substitutes, contrast caution, ask the pharmacist before any new medicine.
9. **VTE prophylaxis follows risk**: none here (young, ambulatory, no risk factor).
