# BRIEF: the quintessential in-class DKA patient (new-onset type 1, young woman)

Built with `.claude/skills/patient-course/SKILL.md`; case study with `.claude/skills/team-case-study/SKILL.md`. Every subagent reads this page first.

## Who and why
- **Woman, about 22, college student. New-onset type 1 diabetes presenting in DKA** (first diagnosis). Weeks of the classic signs (polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, weight loss of several kilograms, fatigue, blurred vision), then 1-2 days of nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and deep fast breathing; a roommate or parent brings her to the ED in the night.
- **The textbook example.** The instructor teaches from **Ignatavicius, Rebar & Heimgartner, Medical-Surgical Nursing: Concepts for Clinical Judgment and Collaborative Care, 11th edition (Elsevier)**. Use its terms, its DKA priorities (fluid replacement, insulin, potassium/electrolytes, monitoring; NCSBN clinical judgment model language: recognize cues, analyze cues, prioritize hypotheses, generate solutions, take action, evaluate outcomes), and its teaching content for a new diagnosis (survival skills: insulin, glucose monitoring, hypoglycemia, sick-day rules, medical ID). Clinical numbers follow the 2024 ADA/EASD/JBDS/AACE/DTS hyperglycemic crises consensus; where the textbook and the consensus differ, follow the consensus and note the difference for the instructor.
- **Separate from all other DKA patients** (`casestudy/cases/DKA_SERIES.md` take-home series and `casestudy/cases/dka_inclass` Caleb Mercer). Do not reuse their names, MRNs, staff or story beats (Maya Thompson is a new-onset 16-year-old: this patient must feel different: an adult, independent, a college student).
- **Quintessential = clean and classic.** Moderate-to-severe DKA, no rare complications. Every classic teaching moment appears once, clearly: Kussmaul breathing and fruity breath, dehydration and the fluid plan, the insulin infusion, potassium falling on insulin (riders, never insulin without knowing K), the dextrose step when glucose falls below 250, the anion gap closing and resolution criteria, the overlap transition to subcutaneous basal-bolus insulin, one low glucose, new-diagnosis teaching and her emotional reaction, and discharge readiness.
- **Order to discontinue:** pick one realistic order the students should discontinue during the 24 h (for example NPO once the gap closes and she tolerates liquids, or a telemetry/cardiac monitoring order after potassium normalizes per protocol, or contact/droplet precautions if a test is negative). Choose what is most natural.
- **At most 2 planted order errors** in 24 h (realistic, teachable).
- No VTE prophylaxis unless a documented risk factor (she is young and will walk).

## Use and timeline
- **In class, about 2 hours.** Class session with synced clocks and the bedside monitor on a second computer. **Also a VR patient**: produce `VR_BRIEF.md` and `vr_states.json` like `casestudy/cases/dka_inclass`.
- **Arrives on the unit at 0700 Day 1 right after the ED.** ED care during the night (labs, fluid boluses, insulin infusion started).
- **Course length: 24 hours, Mon 2027-02-08 0700 to Tue 2027-02-09 0700. Checkpoints every 4 hours: 0700, 1100, 1500, 1900, 2300, 0300, 0700 (7 stops, about 15 minutes each in class).** The case study covers all 7 stops.
- MRN **SIM-40602**; unit: Medical-Surgical Step-Down with telemetry; room **SD-6**. Folder `casestudy/cases/dka_newonset/`. Pre-Built group: **"Case Study Patients"**. caseCode `CS-DKA-NEWONSET-<LASTNAME>`.

## Case study (new style, see the team-case-study skill)
- Keep the charge nurse + 3 pairs format (pairs: **Meds and Orders**, **Vitals & Assessment**, **Results & Labs**).
- **Teach, not grade:** each stop has a short **Quick Check** (2-3 questions: multiple choice or fill-in, NCLEX-style, tied to that stop's teachable moment). The instructor reveals the answers with a one-line "why" at the end of the stop; students correct in a different color. Quick Checks are scored on completion and correction, not on being right.
- More teachable moments: name the one big idea of each stop.

## Rules (from the skill; all apply)
Vitals and bedside glucoses charted only up to the 0700 arrival (the monitor shows vitals later and the instructor says glucoses aloud); a NEW full nursing assessment at every checkpoint; a NEW lab set resulted before every checkpoint; every dose after 0700 is `due`; potassium: peripheral max 10 mEq/h as riders (max 4 in a row), max 20 mEq/L in a peripheral bag, 40 mEq/L central cap, never stack above 10 mEq/h total; at least one ECG with an image; plain language; no real brand/company names except generic (Brand) drug names; unique staff names; no meta wording in the chart; show all the math.
