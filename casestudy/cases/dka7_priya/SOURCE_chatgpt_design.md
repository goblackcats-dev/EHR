**2. Core Pregnancy Glycemic Targets (ADA & ACOG Consensus):**
* **Fasting / Pre-breakfast Glucose:** **70 to 95 mg/dL** (strictly $< 95\text{ mg/dL}$).
* **1-Hour Postprandial Glucose (from start of meal):** **$< 140\text{ mg/dL}$**.
* **2-Hour Postprandial Glucose (from start of meal):** **$< 120\text{ mg/dL}$**.
* **Overnight / Bedtime Glucose:** **90 to 120 mg/dL** (always eat a 25–30 g complex carbohydrate + protein bedtime snack at 2130 to prevent nocturnal hypoglycemia and morning starvation ketosis).
* **CGM Verification Rule:** Confirm any CGM reading $< 70\text{ mg/dL}$, any reading $> 180\text{ mg/dL}$ when feeling unwell, or any symptoms that do not match the sensor value with a capillary fingerstick blood glucose check.

**3. Euglycemic DKA in Pregnancy & Sick-Day Management Rules:**
* **Why DKA Happens at Lower Glucose in Pregnancy:** Because the growing fetus and placenta continuously consume maternal glucose while placental hormones accelerate fat breakdown (ketogenesis) and lower maternal bicarbonate buffering (normal pregnancy $\text{HCO}_3$ is 18–22 mEq/L), **severe DKA can occur in pregnancy at blood glucose levels $< 200\text{–}250\text{ mg/dL}$**.
* **Rule #1 — Never Omit Basal Insulin:** Even if you are nauseated, vomiting, or unable to eat solid meals, **NEVER skip your nightly Insulin Glargine**. Without basal insulin, fat breakdown and ketone acid production accelerate within hours.
* **Rule #2 — Sick-Day Fluid & Carbohydrate Replacement:** If you cannot tolerate solid food, take Ondansetron 4 mg ODT immediately and sip **10 to 15 grams of liquid carbohydrate every hour** (e.g., 4 oz regular electrolyte sports drink, diluted apple juice, regular popsicle, or broth with saltine crackers) plus 8 oz of water per hour so you can continue taking small doses of insulin without dropping your blood sugar.
* **Rule #3 — Blood Ketone Monitoring Thresholds:** Test **blood beta-hydroxybutyrate ketones** (more accurate than urine ketones) every 2 to 4 hours whenever you feel sick, nauseated, have diarrhea/vomiting, or whenever your blood glucose exceeds **180 mg/dL**:
  * **Blood Ketones $< 0.6\text{ mmol/L}$:** Normal.
  * **Blood Ketones $0.6\text{ to }1.4\text{ mmol/L}$:** Call the Endocrinology / MFM on-call physician immediately for sick-day supplemental insulin instructions, drink 8–12 oz of fluids, and recheck in 2 hours.
  * **Blood Ketones $\ge 1.5\text{ mmol/L}$ OR $> 1$ episode of vomiting in 4 hours:** Proceed **immediately to Obstetric Triage** for IV fluids and fetal monitoring.

**4. Fetal Surveillance, Positioning & Obstetric Warning Signs:**
* **Daily Fetal Kick Counts:** Starting at 28 weeks, perform kick counts every day after dinner while resting in the **left lateral position** (lying on your left side increases blood flow to the placenta). Count until you feel **10 distinct fetal movements (kicks, rolls, flutters, or swishes) within 2 hours**.
* **When to Report to Obstetric Triage Immediately:**
  * Fewer than 10 fetal movements in 2 hours (or a noticeable decrease in baby's usual activity pattern).
  * Regular uterine contractions (**6 or more contractions in 1 hour** that do not resolve after drinking 2 glasses of water and resting on your left side), pelvic pressure, lower back ache, vaginal bleeding/spotting, or leaking of fluid from the vagina.
  * Signs of preeclampsia: persistent headache not relieved by acetaminophen, visual changes (spots/flashing lights), right upper quadrant or epigastric pain, or sudden swelling of the face or hands.
  * Unexplained maternal shortness of breath, fruity breath odor, abdominal pain, vomiting $> 1$ time, or blood ketones $\ge 1.5\text{ mmol/L}$.
